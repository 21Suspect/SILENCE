# SILENCE: game design

One document. Numbers are starting values to be tuned in play; nothing here has been playtested yet.

## The pitch

Granny on an island. You wake in a cabin with five days and four ways off the island. Something out there hunts by sound.
It cannot see you. If it catches you, you wake by the same bed the next morning with one day fewer.

Pick a way out, find its parts where this run has scattered them across the island, bring them back piece by piece, and
survive the loudest thing you will do all week: leaving.

## The three rules

1. **It is blind. It hears.** Every action has a loudness. If you make no sound it cannot find you, even at arm's length.
   No line of sight, no hitboxes to argue with: you always know why it found you.
2. **It goes to where the sound was, not to where you are.** Make noise and move. Stay and keep making noise, and it has you.
3. **Light holds it.** The torch stuns it, as now. Lanterns, fires and floodlights make ground it will not cross. All of
   them run out.

## A run

- **Five days.** A day is 24 minutes, as now. The days are scratched on the wall by the bed.
- **By day it sleeps** in its lair. Scout, collect, do loud work. Very loud noise, or noise near the lair, wakes it for a
  while.
- **By night it walks**, starting at the loudest place of your day.
- **Caught, alone:** the screen fades to black, as now, and you wake by the bed at the next dawn with everything you
  carried. The rest of that night is gone.
- **Caught, with friends:** you are out until the next dawn. Until then you watch through a friend's eyes and can talk to
  them; the creature does not hear the voice of someone it has already taken. At dawn you wake by the bed. If it takes
  everyone, dawn comes at once.
- **Sleep:** in the cabin's bed, or a camp bed, you can sleep till dawn if the place is quiet and the door barred. It costs
  the night's hours. If you were loud there that day, it comes to the door.
- **Lost** at the sixth dawn. Then the island is laid out again.
- **Won** by leaving. Four ways out to tick off, then harder rules (three days, no bicycle, a creature that never sleeps).

Dawn, not midnight, is when the taken come back: most catches happen at night, so the wait is a few minutes; coming
back at midnight would make anyone caught after it wait a whole day.

## The Listener

| Noise it hears | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Faint (a step, a door) | Stops, turns its head, drifts that way. Ten seconds of silence and it loses interest |
| Loud (sprinting, a dropped tool, a voice) | Calls once, so you know it heard and how far it is. Trots to the spot and searches it for twenty seconds, listening |
| Very loud or kept up (generator, hammering, an engine) | Runs, and stays for as long as it goes on |
| A second noise while it searches | It turns to the new one. This is how you lead it away, and how you give yourself away |

- **Close up** (3 m) it hears your breathing. Hold your breath (8 seconds, less when winded) and it passes.
- **After searching** it goes back to its round: the day's noisy places, then the roads and paths.
- **The island's size is your time.** It runs at twice your sprint, so a noise 300 m from it buys about 40 seconds.
- **Each day it is worse:** it leaves the lair earlier, searches longer, and from day 4 it sleeps lightly.
- **Tells:** its call when it hears you, clicks while it listens, crows lifting where it moves.
- It cannot be killed or hunted. You mislead it, hold it with light, or are somewhere else.

## Sound and light

| Action | Heard within |
| --- | --- |
| Crouch-walk, whisper, bicycle on a path | 5 to 8 m |
| Walking, a door, a loaded bicycle on a path | 20 to 25 m |
| Talking, sprinting, bicycle on rough ground | 40 to 50 m |
| Bicycle bell, alarm clock, breaking a crate | 60 to 80 m |
| Generator, pump, hammering | 150 m |
| Engine, siren, chapel bell, dynamite, helicopter | the whole island |

- **Weather is cover:** wind cuts every distance by a third, rain by half, a storm by two thirds. The cabin radio gives
  tomorrow's weather. A still night is the worst night.
- **Microphone (optional):** your real voice is in-game noise. With friends it is also how you talk, fading with distance.
- **Light:** torch battery 6 minutes, lantern 15, a fire as long as it is fed. Batteries are found, or charged at a running
  generator, which is loud.

## The whole island, and getting across it

- **Parts are spread over the whole island.** Every way out needs parts from at least three different quarters of it, and
  one lies in the lair. Long hauls are part of the game: the far end of the island must be worth the trip.
- **The board at the cabin** says which area holds each part this run ("spark plugs: around the camp"), never the exact
  spot.
- **The bicycle.** One each, at the cabin. Three times walking speed and nearly silent on roads and paths; slow and loud
  off them. The bell is a lure: ring it and ride. When you wake by the bed, it is back at the cabin.
- **Small parts** go in the backpack. **Heavy parts** (a motor, a battery, fuel) are carried in the hands at a walk, or
  strapped to the bicycle: then it is slower and it rattles, heard at 25 m instead of 8.
- **Parts are banked.** Each way out has a crate at its site; a part put in it is safe for the run. The cabin has a chest.
- **The camp bed.** A rare find. Set it down anywhere in a few seconds and you wake there instead of at the cabin, with
  what you were hauling close by. One at a time; pick it up to move it.

## Four ways out

All four exist every run; the seed moves their parts, the lair and the weather. About five parts each. A fifth can come
later; none is planned.

| Way out | Where | Parts | The finale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The bus** | Bus stop, a tunnel | Dynamite, detonator and wire; the bus's battery, fuel, spark plugs | Blast the rocks out of the tunnel (the whole island hears it), start the broken bus and drive out with it behind you. One drives, the others keep torches on it through the back window |
| **The boat** | A boathouse on one of the beaches this side of the mountains (new) | Outboard motor, two cans of fuel, hull patch, starter cord | Three pulls on the starter before it catches, each heard everywhere |
| **The helicopter** | Relay hut, a landing pad (new) | For the call: fuel and three fuses at the relay hut (today's objective). For the pad: bulbs, cable, fuel for its lights | Call it from the relay hut. It comes at the next dawn and will only land on a lit pad. You cannot be quiet: the helicopter is the noise, and it brings the Listener running. The ring of pad lights is what holds it at the edge while you board, for as long as the fuel lasts |
| **The mine** | Pump house, mine | Pump belt, valve wheel, fuel, a headlamp | Run the pump for ten minutes to drain the lower gallery (a lure of your own making, far from the mine), then crawl out under the mountain in the dark, where every sound carries twice as far |

## Things you find

No crafting tree and no building: things are found and used, parts are fitted at their site. (If that turns out too thin
in play, a few things made from found parts can be added without changing anything else here.)

- **Lures:** wind-up radio, alarm clock (rings after a minute), firecrackers, the bicycle bell.
- **Light:** torch and batteries, oil lantern, flares.
- **Quiet:** felt wraps for boots, an oil can for doors.
- **Doors:** a bar (silent, weak) or boards and nails (strong, loud to put up).
- **Tools:** crowbar, bolt cutters, jerry can, binoculars.
- **The camp bed** (above).

## Friends

- One to four players, one host. The five days are shared.
- It goes for the loudest noise, not the nearest player.
- The taken watch and advise: a voice in a friend's ear that the creature cannot hear.
- Ways out are built to split a group: someone at the pump while the others wait at the mine; a driver and torch-holders
  on the bus; someone on a bicycle ringing it away from the boathouse; someone keeping the pad's generator fed.
- Silent talk for the living: pointing, a wave, marks on the map.

## Questions this settles

| Question | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Caught: what then? | Alone: wake by the bed the next dawn. With friends: watch and talk until dawn, then wake | The cost is the rest of the night, never a walk back or a long wait |
| Drop the backpack? | No, you keep everything | Walking back for it is a chore |
| Parts near their way out, or far? | Far: all over the island | Otherwise most of the island is scenery |
| Long hauls | Bicycle, heavy parts strapped on (slower, louder), banked parts, the board's hints, the camp bed | Long is fine; doing the same haul twice is not |
| Base building, chopping trees | No | It becomes a different game. To be looked at again only if the plain version feels thin |
| Hunt the creature? | No | It stops being a horror game |
| Blind, short-sighted or sighted? | Blind | Hiding is being silent, which the player can always judge |
| Runs at every noise, or only stalks? | Neither: three grades by loudness, and it searches where the sound was | Noise is a budget you spend; moving after a noise always works |
| Having to be loud at a place | Lure it first, work in wind or rain, work by day, or work in bursts | Four answers, each with a price |
| A pick-up it could ignore or camp at | The helicopter is loud and needs a lit pad | You cannot wait it out in silence, and light is the answer to it standing there |
| What lasts between runs? | The four ways out ticked off, then harder rules | Granny's reason to play again |

## Build order

1. **The loop, alone, with one way out.** A blind Listener with the three grades and its call; the lair and its sleep; the
   noise scale; five days and waking by the bed; the bicycle; parts placed by the seed across the island, the board, the
   crates; the helicopter (the relay hut's call exists already; the pad is new). The test: is one run tense and never a chore?
2. **The bus and the boat**, the lures and lights, the camp bed, weather as cover, the days getting worse.
3. **Friends:** host and join by link, voices that fade with distance, the microphone as noise, watching while taken.
4. **The mine**, harder rules, a new island for each seed.
